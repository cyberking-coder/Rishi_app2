# Reply to Apple — External Link Account Entitlement denial

Drafted 27 August 2026, in response to the Developer Relations letter of
the same day denying the entitlement for `com.anuragrishi.knowthyself`
on the grounds that the app "does not qualify as a reader app".

## What this reply is for

**It asks a question. It does not argue.**

That is deliberate, and it is the whole point of sending it. The useful
outcome is not overturning the decision — it is learning *which*
criterion the app failed, because that determines whether any amount of
work could change the answer:

- If the objection is the AI guide's prominence, or how the listing is
  worded, both are fixable and reapplying is worth considering.
- If the objection is that a meditation membership is a subscription
  service rather than a content catalogue, nothing short of changing what
  the product is will move it, and no further effort should be spent.

The stake is higher than it first looked: the entitlement's link lands on
our own website, which sells, so it is the only route by which an iPhone
user could reach a checkout. See the correction in Section 14 of
`PROJECT_LOG.md`.

Arguing the point invites a restatement of the decision. Asking a narrow,
answerable question invites a specific reason. See Section 14 of
`PROJECT_LOG.md` for the full reasoning.

## Before sending

- Send it as a reply on the existing thread, so the case number and the
  app ID stay attached.
- Do not attach a resubmission or a changed build. This is not an appeal,
  and pairing it with changes turns it into one.
- Expect either a specific criterion, a restatement of the original
  letter, or no reply at all. Only the first is worth acting on.

## Draft

> Subject: Re: External Link Account Entitlement — com.anuragrishi.knowthyself
>
> Dear Developer Relations team,
>
> Thank you for reviewing our request and for the explanation.
>
> We accept the decision and are not asking you to reconsider it here. We
> would be grateful for one clarification, so that we can act on it
> correctly rather than guess.
>
> Know Thyself is a library of spoken-word audio — guided meditations and
> recorded talks — together with video series of the same material. All
> content is acquired outside the app, the app offers no in-app purchase
> of any kind, and it contains no purchase flow, price or promotion.
>
> Could you tell us which aspect of the app led to the conclusion that it
> does not qualify? In particular, whether the concern is:
>
>   • that features beyond audio and video playback — for example the
>     in-app assistant that answers questions about the library — mean the
>     listed content types are not the app's primary functionality; or
>
>   • that an app of this kind is not considered to fall within the audio
>     category as the guideline uses it, irrespective of its other
>     features.
>
> Knowing which of these applies would tell us whether adjusting the app
> could bring it within scope, or whether it falls outside the programme
> regardless. We would rather understand the boundary than submit repeated
> requests.
>
> Thank you for your time.
>
> Ritesh Kelkar
> Know Thyself by Anurag Rishi
> App ID: com.anuragrishi.knowthyself

## Why it is written this way

- **It concedes the decision in the first line.** A reply that reads as an
  appeal gets answered as an appeal, which means the original letter
  restated.
- **It states the facts that are checkable and true** — audio and video,
  no in-app purchase, content acquired elsewhere — without claiming the
  conclusion those facts are meant to support.
- **It names the assistant itself.** Not volunteering the most likely
  objection would leave the reply looking evasive to anyone who has opened
  the app, and it is the specific thing worth getting an answer about.
- **It offers two options rather than an open question.** "Why?" is easy
  to answer with a paragraph of guideline text. A choice between two
  stated possibilities is harder to answer without picking one.
- **It asks for a boundary, not a favour.** The last line gives a reason to
  reply that serves Apple too: fewer repeated requests.
